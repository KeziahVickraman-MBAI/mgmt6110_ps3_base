### Finding 1 ~ Search Bar Murkiness
- Where: Search bar at the top of the dashboard (text input + "Lookup" button, above the "Quick test" chips)
- What I did, what I saw: I looked at the search bar before typing to work out what to enter. The field has no label and no visible hint, only the value "AAPL", and nothing says whether it accepts a ticker, a company name, or both. The only clue is the Quick test chips (WMT, AAPL, TSLA, NVDA, BA, CAT), which are all tickers, so I had to infer the expected format rather than being told.

- Which heuristic: <mark>6. Recognition Rather than Recall + 5. Error Prevention</mark>
- Screen or system: `Screen`. It can fix this on its own with what it already has: a label and placeholder (e.g. "Search by ticker, e.g. AAPL") need no new data or backend change. If names should also work, that part needs the system (name-to-ticker lookup), but telling users the accepted format is purely a screen fix.

- Severity, and why: <mark>2 (minor)</mark>. Every first-time user meets this, but the ticker chips give a workaround, so users who know tickers aren't blocked. Users who only know company names are the ones who will stumble.

- The repair: Before typing, a user can tell what the search accepts. The field has a visible label or placeholder stating the format with an example. If names are supported, it says so, and ideally it suggests matching companies while the user types.

### Finding 2 ~ Unclear next step...
- Where: Results page, the **"Synthesize"** button below the price chart and Recent coverage panels, and the stats panel it opens (Price / Coverage / Site)
- What I did, what I saw: I looked up AAPL. The ninety-day chart and Recent coverage loaded, and below them was a **lone "Synthesize" button with no description**. Nothing on the page said what it would do, what data it would use, or whether it would call an AI model, so I wasn't sure whether or how to continue. When I clicked it, a grey panel of stats appeared (Top 6%, 12.7% drawdown, 0 of 5, 5th of 6, and so on) with no heading or summary. Only the fine print at the bottom explained that it is computed from the data on the page with no inference or external model, which is the opposite of what "Synthesize" suggested.
- Which heuristic: <mark>2. Match Between System and the Real World</mark>
- Screen or system: `Both`. 
  - The screen can fix the label on its own: it can rename the button, add a line explaining what it does, and title the panel. 
  - But the screen has no way to describe the output before the click, because it doesn't know what the server will compute or from which inputs. The system needs to supply that: a short summary of each metric, the data it drew on (e.g. 89 daily closes, 5 articles, 1 site), and any warnings (e.g. no coverage in the last 90 days). 
  - The screen can then display that metadata up front instead of a vague verb.

- Severity, and why: <mark>3 (major)</mark>. 
--> The driver is impact: **this panel is the main analytical output of the dashboard**, and a vague label hides it behind a guess. Users who are unsure will skip it and miss the most useful part of the page. Users who click will misjudge where the numbers came from until they reach the fine print.
- The repair: Before clicking, **a user knows what the action does and where its numbers come from**. -> Screen: the button has a plain label (e.g. "Summarise key stats"), a one-line description beside it ("Calculated from the price, news and site data above, no AI"), and a panel with a clear heading. -> System: the server returns a description, input counts, and data-quality warnings for each metric, so the screen can show them before the click and alongside each result. 
* Ideally the server computes the summary **automatically** on lookup, so the button isn't needed.

### Finding 3 ~ Not the latest news...

- Where: Results page, the "Recent coverage · AAPL" panel (The Guardian articles, top right)
- What I did, what I saw: I looked up AAPL and read the Recent coverage panel to see the latest news about the company. The top article, dated 16 May 2026, is a US politics live blog that isn't about Apple. The second article, which is about Apple, is from 22 Jul 2024, over two years old. **Nothing in the panel warned me that the news was out of date**: the heading still said "Recent," and there was no alert or badge. Only after I clicked Synthesize did the Coverage stats show "0 of 5 results published in the last 90 days" and "132 days, age of the most recent article."
- Which heuristic: <mark>1. Visibility of System Status + 9. Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors </mark>
- Screen or system: `Both`. 
  -> The screen can add the alert on its own with what it already has: every article carries a date, and the page already computes "0 of 5 in the last 90 days" and the 132-day age for the Synthesize panel, so it could show a warning in the news panel without any new data. 
  But the screen can't make the news actually recent or relevant. That needs the system: the server should query for articles about the company, sort them by date, filter out irrelevant matches (such as the live blog), and flag when no recent coverage exists.
- Severity, and why: <mark>3 (major)</mark>. The driver is impact on decisions: the panel is labelled "Recent," so users will reasonably trust it and draw conclusions about the company from stale or unrelated news. The problem is also persistent, since nothing on the page corrects the impression unless the user happens to click Synthesize and read the stats.
- The repair: A user can tell at a glance how current and relevant the news is. 
-> Screen: if no article is from the last 90 days, the panel shows a visible alert (e.g. "No coverage in the last 90 days; newest article is 132 days old") and drops the word "Recent" or replaces it with the actual date range. 
-> System: the server returns only articles about the company, newest first, and includes a freshness flag the screen can display.

### Finding 4 ~ No help or documentation to explain the news panel

- Where: Results page, the "Recent coverage · AAPL" panel (The Guardian articles, top right), and the page as a whole, which has no help link or info icons
- What I did, what I saw: I looked up AAPL and read the Recent coverage panel. The top article (16 May 2026) is a US politics live blog, not about Apple, and the only Apple article is from 22 Jul 2024. I wanted to understand why the news looked like this, so I looked for help, but there is no help link, info (ⓘ) icon, tooltip, or "About" section anywhere on the page. Nothing explains how articles are chosen, how recent they are meant to be, or what "The Guardian · Summary Only Licence" means. Because of that, I did not click "3 more," since nothing told me whether those articles would be any newer or more relevant. I also did not rely on the news for my research, because I couldn't tell whether this was all the coverage that exists or a problem with the search.
- Which heuristic: <mark>10. Help and Documentation</mark>
- Screen or system: 
-> Mostly `screen`. It can fix this on its own with what it already has: the rules for the panel (source, search method, licence, what counts as recent) are fixed, so the screen can explain them in an info icon, a tooltip, or a short help section without any new data. The system is only needed for per-search explanations, such as the search term used or why each article matched, which the screen can't know unless the server returns them.
- Severity, and why: <mark>2 (minor)</mark>. The driver is that it stops users from continuing with confidence rather than blocking them: the page still works, but users can't tell whether to trust the news, so they stop exploring ("3 more") and discard the panel. It rises in importance because the news is also stale (see "Not latest news"), and help is exactly what a confused user would reach for.
- The repair: A user who is unsure about the news can find an answer on the page without leaving it. -> Screen: the panel has an info icon or tooltip explaining the source (The Guardian), how articles are matched to the company, what "Summary Only Licence" means, and that results may be older or loosely related. The page has a short help or "About" section covering each panel. 
-> System (optional): the server returns the search term used and a relevance reason per article, so the screen can show why each one appears.

### Finding 5 ~ Intended imagery shown as an error

- Where: Results page, the "Main facility" header: the red "Landsat unavailable — showing basemap imagery." message, the status indicators on the right (Satellite: up · News: up · Price: up), and the "Esri World Imagery" caption under the image
- What I did, what I saw: I looked up AAPL. Above the satellite image, a message in red said Landsat was unavailable and basemap imagery was showing instead. **Red text and the word "unavailable" told me something had failed**, but in the same row the green indicator said "Satellite: up," and the image loaded clearly, showing the Apple Park campus in detail. The only other clue was the small "Esri World Imagery" caption under the image. Nothing explained that **Esri is the intended source**, so I assumed I was seeing a lower-quality backup and didn't know whether to trust the image or wait for Landsat. I didn't look for a retry because I couldn't tell whether anything had actually gone wrong.
- Which heuristic: <mark>1. Visibility of System Status</mark>
- Screen or system: Both. The choice of Esri over NASA Landsat was intentional. Landsat imagery is 30 metres per pixel, so a company site shows as only a handful of blurry pixels, and scenes are often obscured by cloud. Esri World Imagery is a high-resolution, cloud-free mosaic that shows the actual buildings and campus layout, which is what a user researching a facility needs. The screen can fix the presentation on its own: it can remove the red "unavailable" message, stop using error colour, and label the image as "Satellite imagery: Esri World Imagery (high resolution)." But the message comes from the system, which still treats Landsat as the primary source and reports Esri as a fallback. The health check already lists Esri World Imagery as the satellite provider, so the server should stop attempting Landsat, stop flagging its absence as a failure, and return Esri as the source. The site's own description still promises "NASA Landsat satellite imagery," which also needs updating.
- Severity, and why: 2 (minor). The driver is trust rather than blockage: the image works and is better than Landsat would be, but an error message on the first screen makes users think the product is broken or showing second-best data, and it contradicts the green "Satellite: up" indicator right beside it.
- The repair: A user can see what imagery is shown and why, without thinking anything has failed. 
-> Screen: no red error message for an intended source. The image is labelled with its provider and a short reason ("high-resolution imagery so you can see the site itself"), and the status indicators and messages agree. 
-> System: the server returns Esri as the satellite source rather than as a fallback, only reports a problem when Esri itself fails, and the product description reflects the actual source.

### Finding 6 ~ Inconsistent dates and spelling

- Where: Across the results page: the hero ("24 Sept 2026"), the news panel ("16 May 2026", "22 Jul 2024"), the Site stats ("entered by hand 2026-02-10"), and labels such as "Synthesize", "annualised" and "Licence"
- What I did, what I saw: I read the results for AAPL from top to bottom. Dates appeared in three different formats: "24 Sept 2026" (four-letter month), "22 Jul 2024" (three-letter month), and "2026-02-10" (ISO). The spelling also switched between US and UK English on the same page: "Synthesize" but "annualised" and "Licence." The chart header says "Ninety-day close" while its toggle says "90d." None of this stopped me, but I had to reread the ISO date to be sure it was 10 February and not 2 October, and the page felt stitched together from different sources.
- Which heuristic: <mark>4. Consistency and Standards</mark>
- Screen or system: 
-> Screen. It can fix this on its own with what it already has: all the dates are already on the page, so the screen can format them the same way, and the labels are static text it controls. The system is not needed, although the server could also return dates in one standard format to make this easier.
- Severity, and why: **1 (cosmetic)**. The driver is low impact: users can still read every value and complete their task. The mixed ISO date is the only case with a small risk of misreading.
- The repair: Every date on the page uses one format (e.g. "10 Feb 2026"), the page uses one spelling convention throughout, and number-based labels are written the same way everywhere ("90-day" or "Ninety-day," not both).

### Finding 7 ~ No way to close the stats panel

- Where: Results page, the grey Price / Coverage / Site panel that opens after clicking "Synthesize"
- What I did, what I saw: I clicked "Synthesize" and the stats panel appeared below the button. After reading it I wanted to hide it and return to the chart and news, but the panel has no close, collapse or "hide" control, and the button above it still just says "Synthesize," so nothing suggested it could toggle the panel off. The panel stayed open and pushed the rest of the page down. I didn't click Synthesize again, because I couldn't tell whether it would close the panel, recalculate, or do nothing.
- Which heuristic: <mark>3. User Control and Freedom</mark>
- Screen or system: 
-> `Screen`. It can fix this on its own with what it already has: showing and hiding a panel that is already loaded needs no new data from the server.
- Severity, and why: **2 (minor)**. The driver is frequency and annoyance rather than blockage: users can scroll past the panel, but every user who opens it is stuck with it, and the unclear button makes them hesitant to try.
- The repair: After opening the stats panel, a user can easily close it again. The panel has a visible close or collapse control, and the button changes to show its state (e.g. "Hide summary" once the panel is open).

### Finding 8 ~ No shortcuts for repeat research

- Where: Search area (the "Quick test" chips) and the chart's 30d / 90d toggle
- What I did, what I saw: I looked up AAPL and then wanted to check a few more companies and compare their performance over different periods. The only shortcuts are the six "Quick test" chips (WMT, AAPL, TSLA, NVDA, BA, CAT), a fixed list that doesn't include companies I had searched for, and the label "Quick test" reads like a developer tool rather than a feature for me. **I found no recent searches or saved companies**, and the chart only offers 30 or 90 days, so I couldn't look at a longer period such as one year. But longer time ranges need the system: the server would have to request and return more price history than the 90 days it fetches now.
- Severity, and why: **2 (minor)**. The driver is that it affects repeat and expert users, the analysts and students comparing several companies, rather than first-time users, who can still complete a single lookup.
- The repair: A returning user can get back to companies they care about in one tap and view the time range they need. Screen: recent searches replace or sit alongside the fixed chips, labelled for users rather than testers. System: the server supports longer ranges (e.g. 6 months, 1 year) for the chart toggle.
